# Legacy conditional lesson correction

The archived `content/lessons/egiu-038.json` incorrectly treated the second conditional as evidence of impossibility, refusal, or deception. Its theory and dependent scene feedback now explain hypothetical distance without asserting an outcome: an imagined scenario can remain possible, and a tense choice cannot establish whether someone is lying. The detective scene now distinguishes the payment evidence from the speaker's grammar.

The revision also accepts conditional advice such as “If the tests pass, I would deploy it,” distinguishes that recommendation from a speaker's own plan with *will*, and preserves grammatical suggestions with *Shall we …?* while explaining their register. *If I were you* remains the recommended advice pattern without claiming that every use of *was* is impossible in informal speech. These distinctions follow [Cambridge's conditional reference](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/conditionals-if) and the [British Council's explanation of imagined present and future conditions](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/conditionals-zero-first-second).
